This page applies across the Rise group — Rise Financial Group AG and every company under it, including Rise Technologies Oy, which operates the Rise platform and provides the accounting service.

When Rise processes your data, we rely on a small number of trusted service providers. This page lists them, what they do, and where your data is processed — the subprocessors of the Rise service first, then the providers we use to run our own business and website.

We keep this list short on purpose. Fewer providers means fewer places your data can go.

## What a subprocessor is

A **subprocessor** is a company that processes your personal data on our behalf, under our instructions, as part of delivering the Rise service. Under the GDPR you are the controller of that data and we are the processor — so you have the right to know who else touches it.

Your service agreement is with the group company that provides your service — currently **Rise Technologies Oy**. This page covers the subprocessors that company relies on, including other companies within the Rise group.

The first table lists the subprocessors of the Rise service. The second lists providers we use to run our own business and marketing website, where Rise is the controller rather than your processor — none of them receive your accounting data. We list both, because we would rather you see the whole picture.

## Data sources you connect

Rise can read accounting material directly from systems you already use, so that you do not have to forward it manually. You decide which sources to connect, based on how your business actually works.

<Info>
**These sources are not our subprocessors.** They are your own providers, under your own agreements with them. You authorise us to access them, we access only what that authorisation permits, and you can withdraw it at any time — without affecting the rest of the service.
</Info>

**Sources you can connect**

| Source | What we retrieve | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Your previous accounting system** — for example Procountor, Netvisor, Fennoa, or Talenom | Accounting history and supporting documents, so they can be transferred into Rise | Read access with the credentials you provide, for the duration of the transition |
| **Email and calendar** — Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 | Receipts, invoices, booking confirmations and other accounting documents, and calendar entries needed for travel and expense claims | Read only, granted through the provider's own authorisation flow |

**How email and calendar access is limited**

We ask for the narrowest access that lets us do the job:

- **We search for specific documents.** We do not read your mailbox as a whole.
- **We store only what belongs in your accounts** — the accounting documents we find. Everything else stays where it is.
- **We never receive permission to delete, modify, or send.** These privileges are excluded from the authorisation request itself, not merely from our internal rules.
- **You can revoke access at any time** from your own Google or Microsoft account, without going through us.

**Once material is in Rise**

When a document has been retrieved and stored in Rise, it becomes part of the accounting data we process for you. From that point the Data Processing Agreement and the subprocessors listed above apply to it — the same as for anything you upload yourself.

## AI processing

Rise uses AI to read receipts and invoices, propose bookkeeping entries, and flag anomalies.

Inference runs on **Amazon Bedrock in Frankfurt**, using Anthropic's Claude model. This matters for three reasons:

- Your data stays in the EU. It is not sent to the United States for processing.
- Bedrock does not store your prompts or the model's responses.
- The model developer receives no access to your data. Anthropic is the model supplier, not a processor of your information.

Your data is never used to train AI models.

All bookkeeping entries that materially affect your accounts are reviewed by a KLT-qualified accountant before they are posted.

## Changes to this list

You have given general written authorisation for us to use subprocessors. When we intend to add or replace one, here is what happens:

1. **We notify you at least 30 days before the change takes effect.** The notice goes to the data protection contact address you gave us in the service agreement. You do not need to ask for it.
2. **You may object within 30 days.** If you do, and we cannot resolve your concern, either party may terminate the part of the service affected by the change, as set out in the Data Processing Agreement.
3. **Every subprocessor is bound by a data processing agreement** imposing data protection obligations equivalent to our own. We remain responsible to you for their performance as if it were our own.

If a subprocessor has to be replaced at short notice — for example because a provider ceases operations — we will notify you without delay and your right to object is unaffected.

To receive these notices by email, write to us at the address below.
